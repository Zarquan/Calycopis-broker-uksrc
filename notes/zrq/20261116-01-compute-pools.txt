Stage 1's own text contains the first one: its acceptance criterion cannot be met by its changes list.

It says to rename only the three tiers in engine/entities/compute/, then asserts
"a grep of java/src for the old names returns nothing".
Of the 526 ComputeResource references across 69 files, 241 are inside that package
and 285 are outside it, in code the changes list does not mention.
So either the scope grows or the criterion is rewritten.
That is decision one, and five more follow from it.

    SimpleComputeResourceOffer              is the bean returned by the query
    SimpleComputeResourceOfferBean          is the bean returned by the query

==START==
Stepping back a bit :
Is the 'resource' part of the name redundant ?
Rather than renaming SimpleComputeResource to SimpleComputeResourceReservation,
can we simplfy things and rename SimpleComputeResource to SimpleComputeReservation ?
==END==

--START--
Yes — and I think the redundancy is real, not cosmetic. "Compute" is a mass noun here (cores, memory, GPUs);
"compute reservation" reads as one thing, where "compute resource reservation" stacks two nouns that refer
to the same object. The reservation is the compute; "resource" adds nothing.
....
....

My recommendation
Take SimpleComputeReservation and SimpleComputeAllocation, keep SimpleComputeResourcePool and ComputeResourceCriteria.
Adding "Reservation" to fix a name and leaving "Resource" in it fixes the ambiguity only halfway: you would still not know,
from the name, whether the class is the resource or the record of it.

If you agree, I will fold this into the parent plan — decision 1, the vocabulary section, the Stage 1 rename list,
and the Stage 3 allocation name — and then write the Stage 1 implementation plan against the corrected names.
Nothing in the branch needs changing, since no code has moved yet.
--END--
